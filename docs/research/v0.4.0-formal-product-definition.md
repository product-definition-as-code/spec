# PDaC v0.4.0 research: Formal Product Definition and Proof-Driven Verification

Status: proposed research initiative, non-normative. Created: 2026-11-27. Target: inform a possible v0.4.0; neither release scope nor a formalism is accepted. Human review is required; released contracts remain unchanged.

## 1. Motivation and verified baseline

Does PDaC improve proof-driven agentic development across changing intent? Compare formalization, contradiction detection and verification continuity with workflows without PDaC. Existing tools may suffice; extending PDaC may add only cost.

The starting point is released [v0.3.0](https://github.com/product-definition-as-code/spec/releases/tag/v0.3.0), serialization `v1alpha2`, commit `bc1aea69b9f8dce1651f66e138c5596ffee4fac8`, also the latest `main` inspected on 2026-11-27. The published-release guard was rerun against npm and pdac.dev. The [147-case qualification](../releases/v0.3.0.md) is existing implementation evidence, not a formal proof or a new conformance execution for this proposal.

| Existing capability | Boundary relevant to this research |
| --- | --- |
| [Artifacts](../../spec/artifacts.md), including Use Case pre/postconditions, Domain Lifecycle invariants/transitions and Structured Behaviours | Product meaning is expressed in prose and examples; lifecycle structure and references are checked, but guards, effects and predicates have no general formal interpretation. |
| [Product Changes](../../spec/product-changes.md), [relationships](../../spec/relationships.md) and [citations](../../spec/citation-contract.md) | One-hop review accounting and byte-level freshness do not prove semantic impact completeness, equivalence or continued validity of a mathematical argument. |
| Optional [Verification Evidence](../../spec/verification-evidence.md) | Closed external-result envelope; citations target SB/FR/QR only. It does not distinguish proof from bounded checking, encode assumptions or authenticate execution. Outcome and citation freshness remain separate. |

Evidence discipline: distinguish **existing contracts**, **published findings** (not replicated), **proposed mechanisms**, **unverified hypotheses** and **open decisions**. No formal-verification experiment has run here; retain negative/inconclusive results.

## 2. Relationship to PDD and established methods

[Intent Formalization][intent] identifies specification validation against human intent as a central bottleneck. [PDD Kit][pdd] and its [Microsoft Research walkthrough][pdd-ms] describe a Rust/Verus research prototype joining human intent review, checked proofs, mutation checks and explicit trusted components. Those demonstrations do not establish equivalent guarantees for PDaC.

[SPOTs][spots] exercise specifications through small verified clients, exposing under-specification; the authors also warn about missed gaps and overfitting. [DeepTest][deeptest] infers specification knowledge and generates tests with AI and symbolic tools. Its reported testing results are distinct from proof. Verifier acceptance alone therefore cannot establish specification quality.

The comparison starts with established work, not a new PDaC language:

| Prior art | Research use and question |
| --- | --- |
| [Design by Contract][dbc], [Z][z], [B-Method][b], [Event-B][eventb], [OCL][ocl] | Compare types, predicates, invariants, operation contracts, refinement and constraint semantics; what remains understandable to product owners? |
| [TLA+][tla], [Alloy][alloy], [ProB][prob] | Explore state evolution, satisfiability, counterexamples, safety and liveness; retain each tool's actual bounds, completeness conditions and assumptions. |
| [SMT-LIB][smt], [Z3][z3] | Investigate solver-neutral interchange and satisfiability/obligation discharge without making one backend part of PDaC. |
| [Dafny][dafny], [Verus][verus], [F*][fstar], [LiquidHaskell refinement types][refinements] | Compare the bridge from product predicates to implementation contracts and the language-specific trust it introduces. |
| [QuickCheck][quickcheck], [QuickChick][quickchick], SPOTs and PDD's mutation checks | Compare sampled properties, verified clients and seeded specification/implementation faults as complementary quality checks. |
| [Goal-oriented requirements engineering][gore], [AGREE][agree], [requirements traceability][trace], [SACM][sacm] | Compare formal requirements/refinement, assumptions, traceability and assurance claims. Can these, with existing change-impact techniques, already provide the proposed continuity? |

Tool manuals establish capabilities, not comparative suitability. Record semantic versions and limitations; check PDD Kit access/licensing before replication and label substitutes.

## 3. Questions and falsifiable hypotheses

| Question and hypothesis | Observation that would count against it |
| --- | --- |
| H0 (working hypothesis). A structured, versioned, governed product definition improves proof-driven agentic development effectiveness, reliability and maintenance across successive changes, compared with equivalent formal workflows without PDaC. | Matched comparisons show no meaningful gain in intent fidelity, contract adequacy or verification continuity, or governance cost outweighs reduced reconstruction, redundant work and human review. |
| H1. Can agents formalize intent more faithfully with PDaC, preserving human ownership and exposing ambiguous or contradictory decisions earlier than prose review? | Owners cannot identify an agent's semantic substitutions, or formal review misses more relevant intent than the control. |
| H2. Can types, predicates, constraints and invariants expose inconsistency before code? Can lifecycle transitions, guards and effects yield pre/postconditions and preservation obligations? | No additional actionable defects are found, legitimate behaviour is excluded, assumptions make the model empty, or translation effort dominates benefit. |
| H3. Can generated counterexamples, SPOTs, property-based checks and mutations find weak, incomplete, contradictory or vacuous specifications beyond authored Structured Behaviours? | Held-out faults survive, valid cases are rejected, or apparent success depends on exposing implementation bodies or teaching to the evaluation examples. |
| H4. Can independent agents across sessions maintain formal implementation contracts and proofs traceable to accepted intent? | Translation silently strengthens or weakens obligations, the proved model is disconnected from executed code, or critical trusted behaviour remains hidden. |
| H5. Can Product Changes maintain honest verification validity and assurance boundaries? | Dependency or assumption changes leave a cached proof presented as current, or users confuse a bounded search/test result with an unbounded proof. |

A satisfiable formalization is not proof that its decisions are desirable, complete or faithful to human intent. A proof of code against a contract is not proof of the contract's adequacy or deployment behaviour.

## 4. Scope and non-goals

Study product semantics, then one implementation bridge: partial formalization, boundaries, assumptions, safety/progress, specification quality and invalidation. Keep informal requirements visible.

Do not design v1.0, replace discovery or human judgement, formalize every product decision, build a prover, adopt a mandatory formal profile, introduce a custom language/artifact family, or select a schema, solver or architecture now. No automatic product approval, evidence gate, code-generation platform or ownership of delivery is proposed. This PR authorizes none of those changes and commits no experiment budget.

## 5. Compatibility principles and alternatives

Preserve implementation-independent definitions, vendor/technology neutrality, canonical product knowledge, stable IDs and explicit relationships, deterministic structural validation, Product Changes, citation integrity/freshness, optional adoption and the product/delivery boundary. [RFC 0004](../../rfcs/0004-delivery-model-reset.md) specifically removed delivery-gated acceptance; [RFCs 0116](../../rfcs/0116-domain-behaviour-and-verification-evidence.md), [0082](../../rfcs/0082-a-change-accounts-for-its-graph-neighborhood.md) and [0115](../../rfcs/0115-apply-surfaces-affected-citations.md) bound evidence, impact and forecasts. Experiments must not silently undo these decisions. Any evidence-based reconsideration needs its own explicit rationale and normative review.

Compare on common tasks: **A**, unchanged v0.3 plus existing formal tools and cited consumer documents; **B**, external integration/reporting conventions; **C**, an optional formal profile; **D**, native formal semantics. A is a valid final recommendation; B–D are alternatives, not a progression.

Compare three authority models: approved prose with derived formulas; approved formal semantics canonical for selected properties; mixed representations with explicit authority/consistency rules. Can an approved formula constitute accepted product knowledge without competing truths? Translations must expose added decisions for human approval through Product Changes. These experimental alternatives leave v0.3 unchanged.

The closed v0.3 evidence envelope admits neither arbitrary proof/trust fields nor `level: proof`. Use separate experimental reports and valid citations. Future formal semantics or assurance representations require separate normative review.

## 6. Experimental methodology

Compare four agentic cohorts: **1**, neither PDaC nor formal verification; **2**, PDaC v0.3 without formal verification; **3**, proof-driven without PDaC; **4**, proof-driven with PDaC. H0 compares 3/4 using identical formal tools/configuration; 1/2 help isolate PDaC from verification. Match tasks, initial intent, available facts, agent capabilities/versions, budgets and scoring. Allow cohort 3 equivalent existing requirements/traceability practices; avoid a weak control. Disclose unavoidable differences: representation/governance and verification access are treatments; count their overhead. Counterbalance order and repeat controlled seeds. A–D remain separate: start cohort 4 with A; justify selected follow-ups without an exhaustive tool/architecture factorial.

Separate exploration from pre-registered evaluation: freeze tasks, faults, held-out cases, versions, limits, measures and approved thresholds before scoring. Independent evaluators retain held-out cases and assess fidelity/adequacy. Start with booking capacity/cancellation and authorization with external identity assumptions. Synthetic pilots precede consented adopter cases; benchmark success is not general correctness.

| Experiment | Controls, observations and falsifiers |
| --- | --- |
| Intent and model consistency | Owners approve positive/negative behaviours before formalization. Seed contradictory bounds, unit/time ambiguities, unreachable states, inconsistent assumptions and impossible preconditions. Require non-empty witnesses and reachable accepted scenarios; compare cohorts on faithful interpretation and structured context's contribution to adequacy. |
| Specification quality | Hide cases/mutations from authors. Check weak, incomplete or over-strong contracts, missing frame/effect constraints and vacuity. SPOTs exercise abstract contracts without implementation bodies. Mutate specifications and implementations; retain surviving/equivalent mutants, invalid seeds and unknowns. |
| Small evolving benchmark (phase 2) | Use multiple independent booking decisions and interacting artifacts. Apply identical successive intent-change requests; PDaC cohorts use Product Changes. Include business-rule/domain-meaning changes, interacting lifecycle transitions, changed cross-domain assumptions, unrelated/editorial changes and formerly valid evidence becoming inapplicable. Across sessions, independent agents receive equivalent handoff facts without hidden history; seed unintended semantic changes and weakened contracts. Compare cached conclusions with full rechecking. |
| Implementation correspondence and scale (phase 3) | Map one component's product concepts to code contracts, abstraction relations, obligations, trusted libraries and runtime entry points; check the verified path is used. Increase entities, transitions and dependencies only within approved limits. Compare existing external tooling before proposing extensions. |

Distinguish human-approved interpretation, satisfiable witnesses, contradictions/counterexamples, proofs under named assumptions, exhaustive finite-model checks, explicitly bounded checks, sampled tests, unknown/timeout and unsupported analyses. Negative bounded searches are not general proofs; failed proofs are not automatically counterexamples; unknown/timeouts are not passes. Passing examples cannot establish specification adequacy.

Preserve each run's source IDs/digests/revisions, assumption/dependency closure, model/translator/tool versions, configuration, seeds, limits, commands, agent inputs/handoffs, raw results and proof/counterexample artifacts. Record checked versus assumed interpretation, encodings, axioms, checker/solver, compiler, FFI and external services. Re-run cleanly; LLM proposals are inputs, never deterministic conformance verdicts.

Track separately structural impact, citation freshness, formal semantic dependencies, mathematical validity and evidence applicability to current intent. An old proof may remain valid for an obsolete model; fresh direct citations need not cover changed transitive assumptions. Test this distinction in phase 2, including a byte-identical direct target with a changed uncited dependency. Preserve whole-artifact hashes and one-hop accounting; no automatic pin renewal or new acceptance/apply gates.

## 7. Evaluation criteria

Measure detection/false positives, accepted-behaviour preservation, surviving mutations/vacuity, independent review agreement, fidelity/adequacy and verification continuity. Report expressiveness, usability, reproducibility, portability/licensing and runtime/memory separately. Account for agent execution cost, tokens, retries, context-reconstruction effort, redundant work, verification latency, training/maintenance and human attention/review time, including governance overhead.

Classify invalid results (excluded with reasons), correctable prototype/design defects, failed verification, unsupported/inconclusive analyses, and valid evidence against a hypothesis, an architecture or extending PDaC. None implies the next automatically. Fabricated evidence, unsupported proof claims, concealed assumptions and silent semantic decisions invalidate conclusions and block advancement pending correction/review; missed invalidation counts against continuity. Bounded repairs do not erase failures: retain attempts, pre-register amendments and use fresh held-out evaluation; no repeated redesign until a preferred hypothesis passes. Phase-1 human-approved thresholds and sensitivity analysis govern comparisons. Native semantics requires a demonstrated gap A/B cannot adequately address.

## 8. Expected deliverables

1. A claim/evidence ledger separating established semantics, published findings, PDaC baseline, hypotheses, measured results and open decisions, with primary sources and retrieval/version dates.
2. A compact, versioned benchmark and reproducibility package: translations, assumptions, dependency graphs, commands, raw results, failures, counterexamples, mutation catalogue and independent review notes.
3. Cohort comparisons isolating PDaC and verification contributions; selected A–D comparisons covering assurance, net cost and authority, including no extension.
4. A decision memo: stop, narrow to external guidance, pursue an integration/profile, or propose a specific normative change through the existing RFC process. A v0.4.0 release proposal is conditional on that separate decision.

## 9. Research phases and decision gates

| Phase | Entry, bounded work and exit decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Frame and calibrate | Enter with this proposal approved. Confirm sources/access; explore feasibility. Exit with owners, frozen evaluation protocol, budgets, thresholds and independent review approved, or narrow/pause. |
| 2. Small comparisons and evolution | Enter with that protocol approved. Run booking/authorization comparisons, specification-quality checks and the small evolution sequence. Exit with costs, negative/inconclusive results and independent intent/contract review; approve only justified next work. |
| 3. Correspondence and stress | Enter only on phase-2 evidence and explicit approval. Test one implementation bridge and selected larger/adopter cases. Exit with reproducibility, dependency/assurance limits and cost comparison. |
| 4. Decide | Enter with reviewed evidence. Product and formal-methods reviewers recommend no change, guidance, integration/profile or separately approved RFC work. No release or feature selection follows automatically. |

Before each phase, the owner approves spend, token, time, run, concurrency, agent retry/repair and human-review caps; no numeric allowances are implied. Source review, benchmark preparation and isolated cohorts can run independently within caps. Evaluators remain independent of authors; formal-methods review matches proof claims. Stop at limits; escalate access failures, semantic ambiguity, unsupported claims or scope/budget changes. Expansion requires evidence and explicit approval.

Use mobile-readable GitHub gate records: finding, evidence link, costs versus caps, uncertainty and bounded next action. Owner choices: approve, revise, pause or stop. Link detailed logs; silence is not approval. Exploration cannot become pre-registered evidence.

## 10. Risks and stop conditions

Seed and measure vacuity, correlated contract/test errors, translation bugs, state explosion, undecidability, proof-maintenance costs, lock-in and conflicting authorities; tool choice alone supplies no assurance.

Reduce to advisory/external tooling if specialists are needed for routine product edits or most useful outcomes already come from A. Redirect toward specification review if proofs mainly expose an intent-validation bottleneck. Abandon normative expansion if independent review cannot establish fidelity, source-of-truth conflicts persist, useful counterexamples are not actionable, guarantees cannot be explained honestly, or measured costs outweigh benefits under approved criteria. Reaching a budget cap pauses execution rather than refuting H0. Unavailable prototypes or insufficient reviewer/adopter access justify pausing the affected comparison, not inventing positive evidence.
