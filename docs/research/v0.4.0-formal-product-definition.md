# PDaC v0.4.0 research: Formal Product Definition and Proof-Driven Verification

Status: proposed research initiative, non-normative. Created: 2026-11-27. Target: inform a possible v0.4.0; neither release scope nor a formalism is accepted. Human review is required; released contracts remain unchanged.

## 1. Motivation and verified baseline

Can owners progressively formalize accepted intent, discover contradictions before implementation, and preserve verification validity through Product Changes? The alternative is that existing formal tools plus PDaC suffice, and methodological expansion adds cost without useful assurance.

The starting point is released [v0.3.0](https://github.com/product-definition-as-code/spec/releases/tag/v0.3.0), serialization `v1alpha2`, commit `bc1aea69b9f8dce1651f66e138c5596ffee4fac8`, also the latest `main` inspected on 2026-11-27. The published-release guard was rerun against npm and pdac.dev. The [147-case qualification](../releases/v0.3.0.md) is existing implementation evidence, not a formal proof or a new conformance execution for this proposal.

| Existing capability | Boundary relevant to this research |
| --- | --- |
| [Artifacts](../../spec/artifacts.md), including Use Case pre/postconditions, Domain Lifecycle invariants/transitions and Structured Behaviours | Product meaning is expressed in prose and examples; lifecycle structure and references are checked, but guards, effects and predicates have no general formal interpretation. |
| [Product Changes](../../spec/product-changes.md), [relationships](../../spec/relationships.md) and [citations](../../spec/citation-contract.md) | One-hop review accounting and byte-level freshness do not prove semantic impact completeness, equivalence or continued validity of a mathematical argument. |
| Optional [Verification Evidence](../../spec/verification-evidence.md) | Closed external-result envelope; citations target SB/FR/QR only. It does not distinguish proof from bounded checking, encode assumptions or authenticate execution. Outcome and citation freshness remain separate. |

Evidence discipline: the table records **existing contracts**; research citations record **published findings**, not independent replication. Mechanisms and criteria are **proposed**, hypotheses **unverified**, decisions **open**. No new formal-verification experiment has run. Future results retain negative and inconclusive outcomes.

## 2. Relationship to PDD and established methods

[Intent Formalization][intent] identifies specification validation against human intent as a central bottleneck. [PDD Kit][pdd] and its [Microsoft Research walkthrough][pdd-ms] describe a Rust/Verus research prototype joining human intent review, checked proofs, mutation checks and explicit trusted components. Those demonstrations do not establish equivalent guarantees for PDaC.

[SPOTs][spots] exercise specifications through small verified clients, exposing under-specification; the authors also warn about missed gaps and overfitting. [DeepTest][deeptest] infers specification knowledge and generates tests with AI and symbolic tools. Its reported testing results are distinct from proof. Verifier acceptance alone therefore cannot establish specification quality.

The comparison starts with established work, not a new PDaC language:

| Prior art | Research use and question |
| --- | --- |
| [Design by Contract][dbc], [Z][z], [B-Method][b], [Event-B][eventb], [OCL][ocl] | Compare types, predicates, invariants, operation contracts, refinement and constraint semantics; what remains understandable to product owners? |
| [TLA+][tla], [Alloy][alloy], [ProB][prob] | Explore state evolution, satisfiability, counterexamples, safety and liveness; retain each tool's actual bounds, completeness conditions and assumptions. |
| [SMT-LIB][smt], [Z3][z3] | Investigate solver-neutral interchange and satisfiability/obligation discharge without making one backend part of PDaC. |
| [Dafny][dafny], [Verus][verus], [F*][fstar], [LiquidHaskell refinement types][refinements] | Compare the bridge from product predicates to implementation contracts and the language-specific trust it introduces. |
| [QuickCheck][quickcheck], [QuickChick][quickchick], SPOTs and PDD's mutation checks | Compare sampled properties, verified clients and seeded specification/implementation faults as complementary quality checks. |
| [Goal-oriented requirements engineering][gore], [AGREE][agree] | Additional prior art: investigate goal refinement, environmental assumptions and compositional assume-guarantee contracts before inventing equivalents. |

Tool documentation establishes capabilities, not comparative suitability. Extend this initial map with versioned semantics and empirical limitations. Check PDD Kit access, licensing and reproducibility before replication; label substitute experiments accurately.

## 3. Questions and falsifiable hypotheses

| Question and hypothesis | Observation that would count against it |
| --- | --- |
| H1. Can progressive formalization preserve human ownership? Reviewing concrete alternatives and counterexamples may expose ambiguous or contradictory decisions earlier than prose review alone. | Owners cannot identify an agent's semantic substitutions, or formal review misses more relevant intent than the control. |
| H2. Can domain types, predicates, constraints and invariants yield useful satisfiability and consistency checks before code exists? Can lifecycle states, transitions, guards and effects yield preconditions, postconditions and preservation obligations? | No additional actionable defects are found, legitimate behaviour is excluded, assumptions make the model empty, or translation effort dominates benefit. |
| H3. Can generated counterexamples, SPOTs, property-based checks and mutations find weak, incomplete, contradictory or vacuous specifications beyond authored Structured Behaviours? | Held-out faults survive, valid cases are rejected, or apparent success depends on exposing implementation bodies or teaching to the evaluation examples. |
| H4. Can product-level semantics support formal implementation contracts and proofs traceable to accepted intent? | Translation silently strengthens or weakens obligations, the proved model is disconnected from executed code, or critical trusted behaviour remains hidden. |
| H5. Can Product Changes maintain honest verification validity and assurance boundaries? | Dependency or assumption changes leave a cached proof presented as current, or users confuse a bounded search/test result with an unbounded proof. |

A satisfiable formalization is not proof that its decisions are desirable, complete or faithful to human intent. A proof of code against a contract is not proof of the contract's adequacy or deployment behaviour.

## 4. Scope and non-goals

Study product semantics first, then one implementation bridge. Include partial formalization, domain boundaries, assumptions, safety/progress properties, specification quality and proof invalidation. Keep subjective and deliberately informal requirements visible.

Do not design v1.0, replace discovery or human judgement, formalize every product decision, build a prover, adopt a mandatory formal profile, introduce a custom language/artifact family, or select a schema, solver or architecture now. No automatic product approval, evidence gate, code-generation platform or ownership of delivery is proposed. This PR authorizes none of those changes and commits no experiment budget.

## 5. Compatibility principles and alternatives

Preserve implementation-independent definitions, vendor/technology neutrality, canonical product knowledge, stable IDs and explicit relationships, deterministic structural validation, Product Changes, citation integrity/freshness, optional adoption and the product/delivery boundary. [RFC 0004](../../rfcs/0004-delivery-model-reset.md) specifically removed delivery-gated acceptance; [RFCs 0116](../../rfcs/0116-domain-behaviour-and-verification-evidence.md), [0082](../../rfcs/0082-a-change-accounts-for-its-graph-neighborhood.md) and [0115](../../rfcs/0115-apply-surfaces-affected-citations.md) bound evidence, impact and forecasts. Experiments must not silently undo these decisions. Any evidence-based reconsideration needs its own explicit rationale and normative review.

Compare on common tasks: **A**, unchanged v0.3 plus existing formal tools and cited consumer documents; **B**, external integration/reporting conventions; **C**, an optional formal profile; **D**, native formal semantics. A is a valid final recommendation; B–D are alternatives, not a progression.

Identify authored/generated content and who approves meaning. External formal models consume intent without becoming a second product authority. Added meaning returns through human review and Product Change; generated translations remain reproducible and non-authoritative. Study prose/formula disagreements rather than choosing authority by tooling fiat.

The closed v0.3 evidence envelope admits neither arbitrary proof/trust fields nor `level: proof`. Use separate experimental reports and valid citations. Future formal semantics or assurance representations require separate normative review.

## 6. Experimental methodology

Pre-register tasks, faults, controls, versions, limits, measures and thresholds before scoring. Start with booking capacity/cancellation and authorization with external identity assumptions. Add a larger concurrent or time-dependent workflow only after approval. Use synthetic cases before consented adopter cases; small pilots do not establish population-wide benefit.

| Experiment | Controls, observations and falsifiers |
| --- | --- |
| Intent and model consistency | Human owners approve intended positive/negative behaviours before formalization. Seed contradictory bounds, incompatible rules, unit/time ambiguities, unreachable states, inconsistent assumptions and impossible preconditions. Require non-empty witnesses and reachable accepted scenarios as well as invariant checks. Compare prose/SB review with A–D. |
| Specification quality | Hold back examples and mutations from formalization authors. Test too-weak and too-strong contracts, missing frame/effect constraints and vacuous implications. SPOTs prove small client properties against abstract contracts without inspecting implementation bodies. Mutate both specifications and implementations; record surviving/equivalent mutants, invalid seeds and unknown verdicts rather than assuming each is a real defect. |
| Implementation correspondence | On one bounded component, document the mapping from product concepts to code contracts, representation/abstraction relations, proof obligations, trusted libraries, translation steps and runtime entry points. Check that the verified path is actually used. Compare the effort and assurance of an external tool combination before considering PDaC extensions. |
| Evolution and scalability | Apply controlled changes to rules, types, guards, invariants, assumptions, imports and tools. Include an uncited transitive dependency change while the direct target stays byte-identical, and an editorial-only change. Compare cached conclusions with full rechecking; scale entities, transitions and obligation dependencies under fixed limits. |

Verification reports should distinguish: human-approved interpretation; satisfiable instance/witness; contradiction/counterexample; proof under named assumptions; exhaustive checking of a stated finite model; bounded checking with explicit scope; sampled empirical tests; unknown/timeout; and unsupported analysis. A negative bounded search is not a general proof. A failed proof attempt is not automatically a counterexample. Neither solver `unknown` nor a timeout is a pass. Passing examples alone does not establish that all intended obligations were specified.

For every run, preserve source IDs/digests and revisions, the complete assumption/dependency closure, formal-model and translator versions, solver/prover configuration and seeds, platform/resource limits, commands, raw output, proof/counterexample artifacts and limitations. Explicitly record trusted components: human interpretation, encodings, axioms, verifier/solver or certificate checker, compiler/extraction, FFI and relevant external services. Identify which are checked versus assumed. Re-run from a clean environment; LLM proposals are recorded inputs, never deterministic conformance verdicts.

Separate citation freshness from proof validity: existing whole-artifact hashes remain unchanged. A current direct citation does not establish that every proof dependency is unchanged; a stale citation does not itself mathematically refute an earlier proof. Test invalidation and rechecking without automatic pin renewal, redefining one-hop impact, or adding acceptance/apply gates.

## 7. Evaluation criteria

Measure detection/false positives by fault class, accepted-behaviour preservation, surviving mutations/vacuity, review agreement, counterexample latency, proof assumptions, translation fidelity, author/reviewer effort and training, maintenance, reproducibility, portability/licensing and runtime/memory growth. Report unsupported fragments; keep expressiveness, usability and assurance distinct.

Proposed hard rejection criteria: any unsupported universal-proof claim, concealed assumption, silent semantic decision or missed invalidation in the seeded dependency tests blocks advancement. Beneficial detection must improve on the control without excluding owner-approved behaviour. Quantitative detection, false-positive, review-time and compute thresholds require human approval at phase 1, before measured experiments; report sensitivity to those thresholds. Native semantics needs a demonstrated gap that A/B cannot adequately address, not merely a successful toy proof.

## 8. Expected deliverables

1. A claim/evidence ledger separating established semantics, published findings, PDaC baseline, hypotheses, measured results and open decisions, with primary sources and retrieval/version dates.
2. A compact, versioned benchmark and reproducibility package: translations, assumptions, dependency graphs, commands, raw results, failures, counterexamples, mutation catalogue and independent review notes.
3. A comparison of A–D covering assurance, adoption/maintenance costs and source-of-truth handling, including a serious no-extension recommendation.
4. A decision memo: stop, narrow to external guidance, pursue an integration/profile, or propose a specific normative change through the existing RFC process. A v0.4.0 release proposal is conditional on that separate decision.

## 9. Research phases and decision gates

| Phase | Exit evidence and human decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Frame and survey | Confirm source/tool access and related work; approve owners, bounded experiment budget, domains, controls, thresholds and review protocol. Reduce scope if no reproducible comparison is feasible. |
| 2. Run small comparisons | Reproduce the two model studies with negative controls, specification-quality checks and independent intent review. Stop or improve the experiment if the hypotheses are unsupported; do not expand by default. |
| 3. Test correspondence and evolution | Only after phase 2 approval: test one implementation bridge, dependency invalidation, larger models and an adopter trial. Publish inconclusive runs and costs alongside successes. |
| 4. Decide | Review the evidence with product and formal-methods expertise. Choose the smallest adequate outcome, including no change. Only separately approved normative work enters `rfcs/`; merging this research proposal does not select an outcome or publish v0.4.0. |

## 10. Risks and stop conditions

Risks include vacuous assumptions, correlated contract/test errors, translation bugs, state explosion, undecidability, proof maintenance, language lock-in and conflicting prose/formal authorities. Seed and measure them; tool choice supplies no assurance by itself.

Reduce to advisory/external tooling if specialists are needed for routine product edits or most useful outcomes already come from A. Redirect toward specification review if proofs mainly expose an intent-validation bottleneck. Abandon normative expansion if independent review cannot establish fidelity, source-of-truth conflicts persist, useful counterexamples are not actionable, guarantees cannot be explained honestly, or costs exceed the pre-agreed budget. Unavailable prototypes or insufficient reviewer/adopter access justify pausing the affected comparison, not inventing positive evidence.
